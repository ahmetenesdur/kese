Your agent gets the pouch. Never the vault.

Kese gives an AI agent a spending account it cannot argue with. Payments are private — the pool hides sender, recipient and amount — and every one of them passes a deterministic gate first.

    What a payment actually goes through

    The gate is the product. Everything else exists to make its answer safe to act on.

    Caps are absolute

    The per-payment cap is checked before the approval threshold, so there is no dialog that lets an owner wave one through. A limit you can approve past is a suggestion.

    A retry is not a second payment

    Every money tool takes an idempotency key. Models retry tool calls; the same key returns the original receipt and executes nothing.

    Concurrency reserves first

    Parallel payments reserve against the budget inside one database transaction before anything is signed, so two payments that each fit cannot both pass when only one does.

    The keys stay out of the model

    The signing key and viewing key live in server environment only. Every error crosses a redactor before it can become a log line or a tool result.

    Anyone can be paid

    The pool only pays registered recipients, so a Cairo escrow issues claim links for everyone else. Unclaimed funds return to the payer after an expiry block.

    Private from the world, not from you

    The owner's viewing key produces a full spend report, including the payments policy refused — those never reach the chain and are exactly what an audit is for.

    What this does not hide

    A privacy product that overstates itself is the easiest way to get someone hurt. So, plainly:

    • Public

      Money entering and leaving the pool. Privacy starts inside it. Kese has no shield-and-pay shortcut, ever — bundling a deposit with the payment it funds publishes “this address deposited exactly X” right next to it.

    • Public

      Deposits are compliance-screened. The pool verifies a screening signature on-chain before accepting one.

    • Partly

      Claim links show the amount. The escrow pays through an open note, which carries its value in the clear. The recipient stays hidden; the sum does not.

    • Partly

      Distinctive amounts and tight timing weaken anonymity. Round denominations help. A payment of 137.42 is still a payment of 137.42.
